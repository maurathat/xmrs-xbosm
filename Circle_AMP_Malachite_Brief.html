AMP separates dissemination from agreement and specifies the dissemination layer as Best-Effort Broadcast — deliberately semi-opaque, because correctness does not depend on it. But bounded inclusion does: a payload earns the guarantee only when every correct validator holds it. We have an instrument pointed at that layer.

Where this composes

AMP has already removed the duplicate payment: no mempool, each transaction propagated once inside a payload. The remaining cost is the payload broadcast itself — and in AMP's own complexity analysis that term dominates, at O(n²·log n·m) bytes per height.

That analysis assumes gossip at O(log n) latency and O(n·log n) messages. Those are the parameters XBOSM measures rather than assumes. Selective forwarding acts directly on the constant in front of the dominant term.

Average reach is the wrong metric for bounded inclusion

Gossip research reports average eventual reach. AMP needs a per-payload threshold: attestation by all correct validators is what makes inclusion in the next height mandatory. Above f attestations a payload is merely eligible.

So a reach shortfall does not censor a payload. It silently downgrades the guarantee from must be included to may be included — the exact property AMP exists to provide, lost without any validator misbehaving.

Illustrative, at 50 validators, assuming independent per-node delivery:

At 99.99% per-node reach → ~0.5% of payloads miss the all-correct threshold.
At 99.5% → ~22%.

Independence is a simplifying assumption; real gossip failures correlate, so treat these as an order-of-magnitude sketch, not a prediction.

The conclusion against our own headline: eager selective-k alone is insufficient here. The right shape is eager push plus a lazy repair round — which AMP §4.4 already gestures at, with validators exchanging ids of under-attested payloads and rebroadcasting on demand. The metric worth defining and measuring is P(payload reaches every correct validator within the attestation window), per payload, tail included — not mean node reach.

Both reach columns are shown deliberately: the mean drifts only 99.997% → 99.973% across the sweep, while the worst-trial minimum sits flat at 99.50% and breaks to 99.00% at degree 50. That gap is the quantity the section above is about — and it is invisible in the mean.

What we have not measured

  1. Scale and payload size. Our overlay is 200 nodes carrying single transactions; AMP's prototype is 50 nodes carrying payloads. The topology model matches, the parameters do not. Untested at your n and m.
  2. Per-payload certification probability. The harness reports mean final reach. The all-correct-validator threshold above is arithmetic on that figure, not a measured quantity.
  3. BEB-Validity. Selective-k gives probabilistic delivery, which does not strictly satisfy BEB-Validity. AMP's correctness does not rest on it — but the honest statement is that this substitutes a probabilistic guarantee for a deterministic one, and the repair round is what buys it back.
  4. Lazy repair, and adversary. Neither modelled. Selective dissemination below the attestation threshold — AMP §4.4's second attack — is exactly the case a bandwidth-reduced layer must be tested against.

What would make this concrete

  • Peer topology in the AMP prototype. Degree distribution and fan-out policy — our degree-21 anchor is an XRPL default and needs replacing with yours.
  • Payload size and rate. The m and proposer count behind the 10× preliminary figure, so the estimate above uses your parameters rather than ours.
  • Retransmission cost. Is the latency of a payload refetch measured? It sets the entire error budget for reducing dissemination bandwidth.
  • A place in the bottleneck study. AMP names in-depth bottleneck analysis as open. We would like to contribute the dissemination-layer methodology for it.
